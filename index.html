    Ransomware Attack Simulation — Lifecycle of an Attack

    Lab Scope: Conducted in a safe, controlled environment for IST 323: Introduction to Information Security at Syracuse University.

    Overview: Simulated a complete ransomware lifecycle: built a ransomware dropper, crafted a phishing email, triggered the encryption payload, and practiced recovery using private key decryption. This provided insight into both attack and defense strategies.

    Key Takeaways: Gained exposure to malware behavior, phishing, and exploits like EternalBlue. Reinforced the value of backups, patch management, and incident response readiness.

    Ransomware Attack Simulation Report (PDF)

    Using Encryption to Enhance Confidentiality and Integrity (4e)

    Lab Scope: Completed as part of IST 323: Introduction to Information Security at Syracuse University, this lab explored real-world encryption and key management concepts.

    Overview: Implemented symmetric and asymmetric encryption using Kleopatra, OpenSSL, WinSCP, and Linux cmdlets (echo, touch, ls, gpg) to encrypt, sign, transfer, and verify data. Demonstrated the role of encryption in ensuring secure communication.

    Key Takeaways: Strengthened knowledge of cryptographic principles, digital signatures, and hybrid encryption systems. Learned how encryption upholds confidentiality, integrity, and authentication in cybersecurity operations.
